## 1. What is new in v1.7.0

F1005 extends the 6-utility model by **two** new utilities, because a typical home
PV setup has two physical meters:

- The grid operator's **feed-in meter** — measures what the system feeds into the
  public grid. The EEG remuneration is computed from it.
- The **generation meter** at the inverter — measures the modules' total
  production (before self-consumption). Self-consumption and the self-sufficiency
  rate are computed from it.

Both are **optional** in the app: anyone without a PV system sees no PV tiles and
no sidebar entry in the standard settings
(`active_utilities = ['gas', 'strom', 'wasser']`). Activation is via
*Settings → Utilities & billing → Active utilities*.

## 2. Contract model — a deliberate simplification

A classic supply contract has a base price, working price, advance plan and
special payments. For EEG feed-in this makes no sense: the grid operator transfers
monthly or quarterly *based on the actual generation*, not by a plan. There is no
base price and no advances.

**Consequence in the backend:** a PV feed-in contract carries only
`working_prices` (= ct/kWh remuneration with a `from` date) and, since v3.1.0,
`revenue_statements` (credit notes from a direct marketer, §11). Other fields
are ignored by the `ContractService`, even if passed in via the API.

`pv_erzeugung` hard-rejects any contract create with an
`InvalidArgumentException` — the inverter reading is pure statistics. Anyone
entering data there wants to measure self-consumption and the self-sufficiency
rate, not a tariff.

## 3. Electricity balance (main dashboard)

For PV owners the most valuable KPI is not "how much did electricity cost me" and
not "how much did the PV bring me" either, but **the net**.

```
electricity_balance_net = import_cost − feedin_revenue
```

Sign convention:

- **positive** → net cost (on balance you pay)
- **negative** → net revenue (PV brings in more than the import costs)

Endpoint: `GET /api/strom-saldo` — delivers monthly and annual aggregates over
`strom` and `pv_einspeisung` together.

## 4. Self-consumption + self-sufficiency rate

With a **generation meter** in addition, the two industry-standard figures can be
computed:

```
self_consumption_kwh   = pv_generation_kwh − pv_feedin_kwh
self_consumption_rate  = self_consumption_kwh / pv_generation_kwh
self_sufficiency_rate  = self_consumption_kwh / (self_consumption_kwh + import_kwh)
```

- **Self-consumption rate**: "what share of my generation stayed in the house?" —
  larger with an EV, heat pump, battery.
- **Self-sufficiency rate**: "what share of my electricity came from my own PV?" —
  usually 30–60 % for homes without a battery, 60–80 % with a battery.

Self-consumption is clamped to **≥ 0** — with data errors (feed-in > generation)
the value stays sensible. Rates are only computed if the denominator is ≥ 0.1 kWh
(this avoids a meaningless 0/0 display in winter months without generation).

**Only over months with all three meters (since v2.10.0).** The rates of a year
are calculated only over the months in which import, feed-in and generation all
have data (`months_covered`; monthly rows `covered`). If the generation meter was
added in July, the year used to show "self-consumption 0, self-sufficiency 0" —
the feed-in from January to June was set against a generation from July. The
dashboard states the number of months as long as there are fewer than twelve.

**What self-consumption is worth (since v2.10.0):**

```text
savings_eur    = self_consumption_kwh × import working price   (the base price is not saved)
pv_benefit_eur = savings_eur + feed-in revenue
```

The working price is that of the month, weighted by quantity across the
electricity meters. For "has the system paid off?", the savings are often the
larger figure.

Endpoint: `GET /api/pv-summary` — the field `has_generation_meter` indicates
whether the app can compute self-consumption at all.

## 5. CO₂ as "avoided"

Fed-in kWh × `co2_strom` factor is reported as the avoided amount ("CO₂ avoided:
380 kg"). Up to v2.12 the view put a minus in front — next to the word "avoided"
a double negative; the annual report always showed the amount without it. The factor is the same as for the electricity import (since v2.10.0
that of the respective year) — this is a **simplified model calculation** that
does not subtract the PV life cycle (manufacture, transport, recycling of the
modules). Since v2.13.0 the detail view explains this in the ⓘ on the tile (up to
v2.12 in a tooltip).

**Since v2.10.0 for generation too:** up to v2.9, generation appeared as a CO₂
*emission* in the view and the annual report. Now it shows avoided CO₂ (generated
kWh × factor, feed-in included) and no cost tile. In the annual report the
avoided amount appears **once** — at the generation if there is one, otherwise at
the feed-in; the feed-in appears there as **revenue**, not as costs.

**Your own avoidance factor (since v3.1.0).** Under Settings → Utilities &
billing → CO₂ emission factors there is the field **“CO₂ avoided by PV”**
(`co2_pv_avoided`, g/kWh). If it is set, generation and feed-in use it instead
of the electricity mix; empty leaves everything as before. The app deliberately
suggests no value — there is no reliable figure for every system. The German
Environment Agency’s emissions balance of renewable energy gives a guide.

## 5a. Tariff switch for the feed-in

For the feed-in the amounts are revenue: since v2.10.0 the tariff switch and the
retrospective put the offer with the **higher** remuneration first, and a
positive difference is green (`higher_is_better`). Up to v2.9, more
remuneration counted as "more expensive".

## 6. Example data flow

A PV owner with a feed-in and a generation meter sets up:

1. *Settings → Utilities & billing → Active utilities* → activate
   `pv_einspeisung` and `pv_erzeugung`.
2. *Sidebar → PV feed-in → Meters*: "Feed-in meter" with `installed_on` and
   `initial_counter`.
3. *PV feed-in → Contracts*: the EEG tariff with a ct/kWh rate per commissioning
   date.
4. *Sidebar → PV generation → Meters*: create "Inverter south roof".
5. Maintain readings monthly (ideally together with the electricity reading, on
   the same day).

In the main dashboard a new tile **"⚡ Electricity balance"** appears with import,
PV revenue, net balance, self-sufficiency rate and self-consumption rate for the
current year.

## 7. Battery *(v3.1.0)*

A battery usually has two counters in the inverter or the storage system:
charged and discharged. Create both under **PV generation**, with the roles
**“Battery – charging”** and **“Battery – discharging”**; the capacity (kWh)
belongs on the charging meter (meter dialog → “PV system” → “Storage capacity
(kWh)”).

Battery meters do **not** count as generation and not in the sums of PV
generation — otherwise the same energy would appear twice. Self-consumption and
self-sufficiency keep their definition from §4: what the battery releases in
the evening is already contained in self-consumption, because it was not fed
in.

The PV generation view shows the card **“Battery {year}”**:

```text
losses       = charged − discharged
efficiency   = discharged / charged × 100
full cycles  = charged / storage capacity          (only with a capacity)
```

> **Example (made up).** Charged 1,800 kWh, discharged 1,620 kWh, capacity
> 5 kWh: losses 180 kWh, efficiency 90 %, 360 full cycles.

## 8. Plug-in solar *(v3.1.0)*

Since Germany’s Solarpaket I (2024), plug-in solar devices (“balcony power
plants”) may have up to **2,000 Wp** of modules and **800 VA** of inverter
power. They are registered only in the **market master data register**
(Marktstammdatenregister, within a month of commissioning), no longer with the
grid operator. What goes into the grid is taken by the grid operator **free of
charge** — there is no feed-in tariff. An old Ferraris meter without a
backstop may run backwards for a transition period, until the metering operator
replaces it with a bidirectional meter (§ 10a EEG; applies to devices
commissioned since 16 May 2024).

So there is usually **no feed-in meter**, and without one the app does not know
the self-consumption. For that:

1. Create the inverter’s meter under **PV generation** and tick **“Plug-in
   solar device (balcony system, no feed-in meter)”** in the meter dialog.
2. Under Settings → Utilities & billing → **Photovoltaics** enter the share
   **“Assumed self-consumption”**, for example 70 %.

The app then calculates `self-consumption = generation × share` and from it
savings and self-sufficiency; the PV generation view adds “Plug-in solar:
self-consumption assumed at …% of generation (settings)”. This is an
**assumption**, not a measurement. As soon as there is a feed-in meter — also
the feed-in register of a bidirectional meter, if you can read it — the app
calculates with it and the assumption no longer applies.

What the device changes in your grid purchase is measured by the baseline date
“Plug-in solar in operation” on the electricity meter
([Flat scenario](07-szenario-wohnung.md#7-special-case-plug-in-solar)).

## 9. Payback *(v3.1.0)*

In the generation meter dialog, under **“PV system”**, there are the fields
**“Investment (incl. VAT)”** and **“In operation since”**. With them the PV
generation view shows the card **“Payback”**:

```text
benefit = avoided purchase (self-consumption × unit price) + feed-in revenue
          since commissioning
years   = investment / benefit of the last 12 months
```

If the investment has already come back, it says “Paid back since” with the
month; otherwise “Paid back probably”, projected with the benefit of the last
twelve months.

> **Example (made up).** A plug-in solar device for €800 saves €160 a year:
> about 5 years.

Maintenance, a new inverter and interest are not included. With several
generation meters all investments count together, from the earliest
commissioning.

## 10. Negative wholesale prices *(v3.1.0)*

For systems commissioned **on or after 25 Feb 2025**, § 51 EEG removes the
feed-in tariff for periods with a negative wholesale price — for systems below
100 kW from the calendar year after a smart metering system is installed. A
longer funding period partly offsets this (§ 51a EEG). If commissioning (“In
operation since”, with several generation meters the earliest) falls on or
after that day and the country is Germany, the PV generation view points this
out. The app cannot calculate the lost hours — that would need quarter-hour
values.

## 11. Credit notes from the direct marketer *(v3.1.0)*

Whoever sells their electricity through a direct marketer does not get a fixed
rate per kWh but credit notes that vary with the market value. In the feed-in
contract dialog you enter them under **“Credit notes from the direct
marketer”**: From, “To (inclusive)”, amount. A credit note replaces kWh ×
feed-in tariff for its period, spread over the months day by day; for the
remaining days the contract’s rate still applies.

> **Example (made up).** A credit note for June 2026 of €23.40 appears in June
> exactly like that, however many kWh the rate would have given.
