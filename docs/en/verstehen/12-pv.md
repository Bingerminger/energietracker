## 1. What is new in v1.7.0

F1005 extends the 6-utility model by **two** new utilities, because a typical home
PV setup has two physical meters:

- The grid operator's **feed-in meter** — measures what the system feeds into the
  public grid. The EEG remuneration is computed from it.
- The **generation meter** at the inverter — measures the modules' total
  production (before self-consumption). Self-consumption and the self-sufficiency
  rate are computed from it.

Both are **optional** in the app: anyone without a PV system sees no PV tiles and
no sidebar entry in the standard settings
(`active_utilities = ['gas', 'strom', 'wasser']`). Activation is via
*Household → Utilities → Active utilities*.

## 2. Contract model — a deliberate simplification

A classic supply contract has a base price, working price, advance plan and
special payments. For EEG feed-in this makes no sense: the grid operator transfers
monthly or quarterly *based on the actual generation*, not by a plan. There is no
base price and no advances.

**Consequence in the backend:** a PV feed-in contract carries only
`working_prices` (= ct/kWh remuneration with a `from` date) and, since v3.1.0,
`revenue_statements` (credit notes from a direct marketer, §11). Other fields
are ignored by the `ContractService`, even if passed in via the API.

<!-- doc-claim: feed-in-billing-year -->
**Balance per billing year** *(v3.12.0)*: the balance card of the feed-in
counts like an electricity or gas contract without an entered bill, over the
**running billing year** (anchor `billing_cycle_anchor_pv_einspeisung`,
default 01-01), not since commissioning. Example (made up): 150 kWh fed in per
month at 8.0 ct, read up to 1 October: **€108.00** for nine months of the
year. Up to v3.11 the card showed the remuneration of every year since
commissioning as "credit with the grid operator".

`pv_erzeugung` hard-rejects any contract create with an
`InvalidArgumentException` — the inverter reading is pure statistics. Anyone
entering data there wants to measure self-consumption and the self-sufficiency
rate, not a tariff.

## 3. Electricity balance (main dashboard)

For PV owners the most valuable KPI is not "how much did electricity cost me" and
not "how much did the PV bring me" either, but **the net**.

```
electricity_balance_net = import_cost − feedin_revenue
```

Sign convention:

- **positive** → net cost (on balance you pay)
- **negative** → net revenue (PV brings in more than the import costs)

Endpoint: `GET /api/strom-saldo` — delivers monthly and annual aggregates over
`strom` and `pv_einspeisung` together.

## 4. Self-consumption + self-sufficiency rate

With a **generation meter** in addition, the two industry-standard figures can be
computed:

```
self_consumption_kwh   = pv_generation_kwh − pv_feedin_kwh
self_consumption_rate  = self_consumption_kwh / pv_generation_kwh
self_sufficiency_rate  = 1 − grid power in the house / house consumption   (energy flow, see below)
                       = self_consumption_kwh / (self_consumption_kwh + import_kwh)   without a battery
```

- **Self-consumption rate**: "what share of my generation stayed in the house?" —
  larger with an EV, heat pump, battery.
- **Self-sufficiency rate**: "what share of my electricity did not come from the
  grid?" — usually 30–60 % for homes without a battery, 60–80 % with a battery.

**Energy flow (since v3.4.0).** The key figure and the explainer “Energy flow
in the house” use the same formula (`PvSummaryService::flows()`), per month
from the amounts; a year is the sum of its months. As in Home Assistant, solar
charges the battery first and the grid supplied the rest of the charge:

```text
Solar → Grid         = feed-in
Solar → Battery      = min(charged, generation − feed-in)
Grid → Battery       = charged − Solar → Battery
Solar → House        = generation − feed-in − Solar → Battery
Battery → House      = discharged
Grid → House         = max(0, import − Grid → Battery)
House consumption    = Solar → House + Battery → House + Grid → House
Grid power in house  = min(import, Grid → House + min(Grid → Battery, Battery → House))
Self-sufficiency     = 1 − grid power in house / house consumption
```

> **Correction in v3.4.0.** Up to v3.3 self-sufficiency was
> `self_consumption / (self_consumption + import)` with a battery too. That
> counted the battery losses as used in the house — they never reach it. With
> a battery the rate therefore drops slightly (sample household “Own house with
> heat pump and solar”, 2025: 39.1 % → 38.2 %); without a battery nothing changes.
> If the battery is charged from the grid, that power still counts as grid
> power after the detour through the battery.

Solar → Battery and Grid → Battery are calculated from monthly totals, not
measured: the meters do not say where the charge came from. The explainer
draws them dashed.

**Day film (since v3.5.0).** With values per quarter hour the same formula runs
per quarter hour — the picture shows what flowed in that quarter hour. The
day's totals (and what has built up by a given time) come from the amounts,
as for a month: if the battery charges from the grid at night and discharges
in the evening, that power stays grid power. Gaps do not count, not even as 0
([Views](../referenz/ansichten.md#1-overview-dashboard),
[API](../referenz/api.md#day-film-energy-flow-per-quarter-hour-v350)).

Self-consumption is clamped to **≥ 0** — with data errors (feed-in > generation)
the value stays sensible. Rates are only computed if the denominator is ≥ 0.1 kWh
(this avoids a meaningless 0/0 display in winter months without generation).

**Only over months with all three meters (since v2.10.0).** The rates of a year
are calculated only over the months in which import, feed-in and generation all
have data (`months_covered`; monthly rows `covered`). If the generation meter was
added in July, the year used to show "self-consumption 0, self-sufficiency 0" —
the feed-in from January to June was set against a generation from July. The
dashboard states the number of months as long as there are fewer than twelve.

**What self-consumption is worth (since v2.10.0):**

```text
savings_eur    = self_consumption_kwh × import working price   (the base price is not saved)
pv_benefit_eur = savings_eur + feed-in revenue
```

The working price is that of the month, weighted by quantity across the
electricity meters. For "has the system paid off?", the savings are often the
larger figure.

**Few readings (since v3.7.0).** A generation meter with two readings gives
every month the same daily rate — December like July. Only the total between the
readings is known then; how it splits over the months, and with it
self-consumption, self-sufficiency, savings per month and the payback curve, are
estimates. The PV summary says so with the number of readings, the monthly rows
carry `interpolated` (table “≈”, chart hatched); the values themselves stay as
they are. Interpolated means: from a reading interval of more than 45 days
([Basics §1](00-overview.md#measured-or-interpolated-since-v370)).

**Measured feed-in is never capped (since v3.9.0).** Up to v3.7 the energy flow
used `sun → grid = min(feed-in, generation)`. If generation is spread from a long
interval, in June it is below what the system really generated, and the measured
feed-in was cut down to it: in the messy test household 2024 showed “↑1,165 kWh”
in the figure next to 1,197 kWh feed-in in tile and table. Now `sun → grid` is
always the measured feed-in; the sun in the figure then shows at least that. A
month with more feed-in than generation is **contradictory**
(`feed_above_generation`, more than 0.5 kWh above): it carries “!” in the table,
and the figure names it.

**The same period (since v3.9.0).** If a series covers a month on fewer than 90 %
of the days of the longest series (generation up to the 15th, feed-in the whole
month), the month does not count as covered (`partial_series`, table “*”): self
consumption would be half a month of sun against a whole month of feed-in. The
figure “Energy flow in the house” names the covered period (`flow_period`:
“2025 (Jan to Jul, 7 months)”), puts “≈” in front of the estimated amounts for
interpolated months and says in the sentence below which series is interpolated.

**Annual report (since v3.9.0).** The solar block takes every amount from the
period of the energy flow (sun, self-used, fed in, grid: a total that adds up),
names the period when it is not a whole year, and compares with the previous year
only when both years have twelve covered months and generation and feed-in were
read at the turns of the year; otherwise a note says why the comparison is
missing. If the block rests on interpolated months, the benefit is called
“estimated”, not “measured”.

Endpoint: `GET /api/pv-summary` — the field `has_generation_meter` indicates
whether the app can compute self-consumption at all.

## 5. CO₂ as "avoided"

Fed-in kWh × `co2_strom` factor is reported as the avoided amount ("CO₂ avoided:
380 kg"). Up to v2.12 the view put a minus in front — next to the word "avoided"
a double negative; the annual report always showed the amount without it. The factor is the same as for the electricity import (since v2.10.0
that of the respective year) — this is a **simplified model calculation** that
does not subtract the PV life cycle (manufacture, transport, recycling of the
modules). Since v2.13.0 the detail view explains this in the ⓘ on the tile (up to
v2.12 in a tooltip).

**Since v2.10.0 for generation too:** up to v2.9, generation appeared as a CO₂
*emission* in the view and the annual report. Now it shows avoided CO₂ (generated
kWh × factor, feed-in included) and no cost tile. In the annual report the
avoided amount appears **once** — at the generation if there is one, otherwise at
the feed-in; the feed-in appears there as **revenue**, not as costs.

**Your own avoidance factor (since v3.1.0).** Under Settings →
Calculation → CO₂ emission factors there is the field **“CO₂ avoided by PV”**
(`co2_pv_avoided`, g/kWh). If it is set, generation and feed-in use it instead
of the electricity mix; empty leaves everything as before. The app deliberately
suggests no value — there is no reliable figure for every system. The German
Environment Agency’s emissions balance of renewable energy gives a guide.

## 5a. Tariff switch for the feed-in

For the feed-in the amounts are revenue: since v2.10.0 the tariff switch and the
retrospective put the offer with the **higher** remuneration first, and a
positive difference is green (`higher_is_better`). Up to v2.9, more
remuneration counted as "more expensive".

## 6. Example data flow

A PV owner with a feed-in and a generation meter sets up:

1. *Household → Utilities → Active utilities* → activate
   `pv_einspeisung` and `pv_erzeugung`.
2. *Household → Meters → PV feed-in*: "Feed-in meter" with `installed_on` and
   `initial_counter`.
3. *PV feed-in → Contracts*: the EEG tariff with a ct/kWh rate per commissioning
   date.
4. *Household → Meters → PV generation*: create "Inverter south roof".
5. Maintain readings monthly (ideally together with the electricity reading, on
   the same day).

In the main dashboard a new tile **"Electricity balance"** appears with import,
PV revenue, net balance, self-sufficiency rate and self-consumption rate for the
current year.

## 7. Battery *(v3.1.0)*

A battery usually has two counters in the inverter or the storage system:
charged and discharged. Create both under **PV generation**, with the roles
**“Battery (charging)”** and **“Battery (discharging)”**; the capacity (kWh)
belongs on the charging meter (meter dialog → “PV system” → “Storage capacity
(kWh)”).

Battery meters do **not** count as generation and not in the sums of PV
generation — otherwise the same energy would appear twice. Self-consumption
keeps its definition from §4: what the battery releases in the evening is
already contained in it, because it was not fed in. Since v3.4.0
self-sufficiency uses the energy flow from §4: the battery losses do not count
as used in the house.

The PV generation view shows the card **“Battery {year}”**:

```text
losses       = charged − discharged
efficiency   = discharged / charged × 100
full cycles  = charged / storage capacity          (only with a capacity)
```

> **Example (made up).** Charged 1,800 kWh, discharged 1,620 kWh, capacity
> 5 kWh: losses 180 kWh, efficiency 90 %, 360 full cycles.

## 8. Plug-in solar *(v3.1.0)*

Since Germany’s Solarpaket I (2024), plug-in solar devices (“balcony power
plants”) may have up to **2,000 Wp** of modules and **800 VA** of inverter
power. They are registered only in the **market master data register**
(Marktstammdatenregister, within a month of commissioning), no longer with the
grid operator. What goes into the grid is taken by the grid operator **free of
charge** — there is no feed-in tariff. An old Ferraris meter without a
backstop may run backwards for a transition period, until the metering operator
replaces it with a bidirectional meter (§ 10a EEG; applies to devices
commissioned since 16 May 2024).

So there is usually **no feed-in meter**, and without one the app does not know
the self-consumption. For that:

1. Create the inverter’s meter under **PV generation** and tick **“Plug-in
   solar device (balcony system, no feed-in meter)”** in the meter dialog.
2. Under Household → Utilities → **Photovoltaics** enter the share
   **“Assumed self-consumption”**, for example 70 %.

The app then calculates `self-consumption = generation × share` and from it
savings and self-sufficiency; the PV generation view adds “Plug-in solar:
self-consumption assumed at …% of generation (settings)”. This is an
**assumption**, not a measurement. As soon as there is a feed-in meter — also
the feed-in register of a bidirectional meter, if you can read it — the app
calculates with it and the assumption no longer applies.

What the device changes in your grid purchase is measured by the baseline date
“Plug-in solar in operation” on the electricity meter
([Flat scenario](07-szenario-wohnung.md#7-special-case-plug-in-solar)).

## 9. Payback *(v3.1.0)*

In the generation meter dialog, under **“PV system”**, there are the fields
**“Investment (incl. VAT)”** and **“In operation since”**. With them the PV
generation view shows the card **“Payback”**:

```text
benefit = avoided purchase (self-consumption × unit price) + feed-in revenue
          since commissioning
years   = investment / benefit of the last 12 months
```

If the investment has already come back, it says “Paid back since” with the
month; otherwise “Paid back probably”, projected with the benefit of the last
twelve months.

> **Example (made up).** A plug-in solar device for €800 saves €160 a year:
> about 5 years.

Maintenance, a new inverter and interest are not included. With several
generation meters all investments count together, from the earliest
commissioning.

## 10. Negative wholesale prices *(v3.1.0)*

For systems commissioned **on or after 25 Feb 2025**, § 51 EEG removes the
feed-in tariff for periods with a negative wholesale price — for systems below
100 kW from the calendar year after a smart metering system is installed. A
longer funding period partly offsets this (§ 51a EEG). If commissioning (“In
operation since”, with several generation meters the earliest) falls on or
after that day and the country is Germany, the PV generation view points this
out. The app cannot calculate the lost hours — that would need quarter-hour
values.

## 11. Credit notes from the direct marketer *(v3.1.0)*

Whoever sells their electricity through a direct marketer does not get a fixed
rate per kWh but credit notes that vary with the market value. In the feed-in
contract dialog you enter them under **“Credit notes from the direct
marketer”**: From, “To (inclusive)”, amount. A credit note replaces kWh ×
feed-in tariff for its period, spread over the months day by day; for the
remaining days the contract’s rate still applies.

> **Example (made up).** A credit note for June 2026 of €23.40 appears in June
> exactly like that, however many kWh the rate would have given.

## 12. Sun by the weather *(v3.13.0)*

**The app now fetches the solar radiation.** Along with the temperature, the
weather sync brings the solar radiation per day (Open-Meteo, kWh/m²), and the
climate normal the mean radiation per month from 30 years
([glossary](09-glossar.md)).

<!-- doc-claim: pv-weather -->
**Feed-in balance:** “Expected at settlement” is calculated by the solar radiation.
The days since the last reading count with the measured radiation, the next days
with the forecast, the rest of the billing year with the climate normal. How much
your system feeds in per kWh/m² of radiation the app learns from the intervals
between your readings of the last twelve months (if they cover six months, otherwise from all), **one value per season** (a
south-facing roof gets more out of each kWh/m² in winter than in summer). **Until
v3.12 the days between the last reading and today were missing**, and the
average of the read months applied to every remaining month.

**Forecast:** The forecast of generation and feed-in still follows the seasonal
profile of the measured years. A calculation by the solar radiation is built but
has not yet passed its check on a made-up test household. The forecast says so in
a note, and the workshop still uses the base year as measured.

**The climate normal** carries the radiation with its trend: the sun has increased
over the last decades, and the flat 30-year mean was on average 3.4 % too low for
the next year ([measurement](../entwicklung/messung-einstrahlung.md)). If the normal
has no radiation, the balance uses the average of your own days.

**When not:** without solar radiation in the weather data or with fewer than **two
readings at least six months apart**, the balance is calculated as before.

**Limit:** between two readings the app still spreads the amount evenly over the
days. Spreading it by the radiation follows with v3.14.

## 13. Recording payouts *(v3.13.0)*

The balance card of the feed-in calculates per billing year what the grid
operator owes you for the energy fed in. **What it has actually transferred
you enter at the card:** button **“Record payout”**, then **“Credited on”**,
**“Amount”** and, if the payout settles a period, **“Period from (optional)”**
and **“to (inclusive)”**.

The card then shows three figures for the billing year:

- **“Feed-in payment (as of today)”:** what is expected up to today.
- **“Already paid out”:** the sum of the recorded payouts.
- **“Still outstanding”:** expected minus paid out. If more was paid out than
  expected, it says “Repayment due”.

“Expected at settlement” deducts the payouts as well.

**Which payout counts in which year:**

- **With a period**, a payout counts pro rata by the days that fall into the
  billing year. An annual settlement for the previous year that only arrives in
  February thus counts for the previous year.
- **Without a period**, the credit date counts.
- A payout dated in the future only counts from that day.

> **Example (made up).** The billing year starts on 1 January. On 31.03.2026,
> €120 arrive for July 2025 to June 2026: for 2026, 181 of 365 days count, so
> **€59.51**. The annual settlement for 2025 of €85, credited on 15.02.2026,
> counts for 2025 and not in the card for 2026.

All recorded payouts are listed below the card. **“Delete”** removes one;
“Undo” re-creates it. Every recorded payout also appears in the history.

## 14. A new battery in the history *(v3.13.0)*

**When a battery is added, the months before it describe a different household:**
without a battery, more went into the grid and more came from the grid in the
evening. The forecast repeats the measured months, so after the installation it was
too high until the new months prevailed (with the median of three years, for up to
two years).

<!-- doc-claim: battery-arrival -->
**Since v3.13.0 the forecast adjusts the feed-in of the months before it by the charging**
of the battery: as much as it charged in the same calendar month, and for calendar
months it has not measured yet, as much per day as the average of the measured months
(assumption: a battery charges about once a day, limited by the consumption at night
in summer and by the surplus in winter). It needs the charging meter (“Battery
(charging)” under PV generation) with at least **three months** after the installation;
for the import, the discharging meter counts. Measured on the test household: in the
year after the installation, the feed-in forecast was off by 36 to 41% on average, now
by 15 to 19%.

**The import still uses the months before it.** Adjusting it by the discharging is
built but did not pass its check on the test household: at the front meter of a
cascade, the battery takes sun away from the heat pump, so the discharging does not
fully replace the import there. The forecast says so in a note and suggests a
**baseline date on the day of the installation** ([glossary: baseline date](09-glossar.md)):
then the meter only uses the months after it. The same note appears while the battery
has measured less than three months or the meter for that side is missing. The notes
stay for **two years** after the installation; after that the new months prevail.

**The installation is the day set on the battery meter** (meter dialog, installation of
the device). If you add the meters for a battery that has been running for a while,
enter its installation date there: otherwise the app takes the day you added them as
the installation and also subtracts the charging from months that already had the
battery. The note names the date the app uses.

Monthly rows, analysis and annual report stay as measured; only what the forecast
learns from the months is adjusted. A baseline date from the installation makes the
adjustment unnecessary.
