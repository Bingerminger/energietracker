## 1. What is new in v1.7.0

F1005 extends the 6-utility model by **two** new utilities, because a typical home
PV setup has two physical meters:

- The grid operator's **feed-in meter** — measures what the system feeds into the
  public grid. The EEG remuneration is computed from it.
- The **generation meter** at the inverter — measures the modules' total
  production (before self-consumption). Self-consumption and the self-sufficiency
  rate are computed from it.

Both are **optional** in the app: anyone without a PV system sees no PV tiles and
no sidebar entry in the standard settings
(`active_utilities = ['gas', 'strom', 'wasser']`). Activation is via
*Settings → Active utilities*.

## 2. Contract model — a deliberate simplification

A classic supply contract has a base price, working price, advance plan and
special payments. For EEG feed-in this makes no sense: the grid operator transfers
monthly or quarterly *based on the actual generation*, not by a plan. There is no
base price and no advances.

**Consequence in the backend:** a PV feed-in contract carries only
`working_prices` (= ct/kWh remuneration with a `from` date). Other fields are
ignored by the `ContractService`, even if passed in via the API.

`pv_erzeugung` hard-rejects any contract create with an
`InvalidArgumentException` — the inverter reading is pure statistics. Anyone
entering data there wants to measure self-consumption and the self-sufficiency
rate, not a tariff.

## 3. Electricity balance (main dashboard)

For PV owners the most valuable KPI is not "how much did electricity cost me" and
not "how much did the PV bring me" either, but **the net**.

```
electricity_balance_net = import_cost − feedin_revenue
```

Sign convention:

- **positive** → net cost (on balance you pay)
- **negative** → net revenue (PV brings in more than the import costs)

Endpoint: `GET /api/strom-saldo` — delivers monthly and annual aggregates over
`strom` and `pv_einspeisung` together.

## 4. Self-consumption + self-sufficiency rate

With a **generation meter** in addition, the two industry-standard figures can be
computed:

```
self_consumption_kwh   = pv_generation_kwh − pv_feedin_kwh
self_consumption_rate  = self_consumption_kwh / pv_generation_kwh
self_sufficiency_rate  = self_consumption_kwh / (self_consumption_kwh + import_kwh)
```

- **Self-consumption rate**: "what share of my generation stayed in the house?" —
  larger with an EV, heat pump, battery.
- **Self-sufficiency rate**: "what share of my electricity came from my own PV?" —
  usually 30–60 % for homes without a battery, 60–80 % with a battery.

Self-consumption is clamped to **≥ 0** — with data errors (feed-in > generation)
the value stays sensible. Rates are only computed if the denominator is ≥ 0.1 kWh
(this avoids a meaningless 0/0 display in winter months without generation).

Endpoint: `GET /api/pv-summary` — the field `has_generation_meter` indicates
whether the app can compute self-consumption at all.

## 5. CO₂ as "avoided"

Fed-in kWh × `co2_strom` factor is reported as a **negative** value ("−380 kg CO₂
avoided"). The factor is the same as for the electricity import — this is a
**simplified model calculation** that does not subtract the PV life cycle
(manufacture, transport, recycling of the modules). The detail view carries a
tooltip with this note.

## 6. Example data flow

A PV owner with a feed-in and a generation meter sets up:

1. *Settings* → activate `pv_einspeisung` and `pv_erzeugung`.
2. *Sidebar → PV feed-in → Meters*: "Feed-in meter" with `installed_on` and
   `initial_counter`.
3. *PV feed-in → Contracts*: the EEG tariff with a ct/kWh rate per commissioning
   date.
4. *Sidebar → PV generation → Meters*: create "Inverter south roof".
5. Maintain readings monthly (ideally together with the electricity reading, on
   the same day).

In the main dashboard a new tile **"⚡ Electricity balance"** appears with import,
PV revenue, net balance, self-sufficiency rate and self-consumption rate for the
current year.
